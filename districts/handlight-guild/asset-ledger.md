# Guild Asset Ledger

**District:** The Handlight Guild
**Origin Name:** Future Hands
**Document Type:** Asset Inventory
**Wuerld Translation:** A ledger of the people, devices, systems, records, rooms, and anchors the Guild must protect

---

## Purpose of This Ledger

The Handlight Guild cannot protect what it has not named.

This ledger is the first record of the Guild’s important assets: the things that keep the training hall alive, the students learning, the staff working, the systems running, and the doors from swinging open to the wrong people.

In regular cybersecurity language, this is an **asset inventory**.

In Watertowne Wuerld language, this is a list of the Guild’s **anchors**.

Some anchors are machines.
Some are accounts.
Some are records.
Some are rooms.
Some are people.
Some are systems everyone forgets about until they break.

If the Guild loses track of its anchors, the Guild loses track of its risk.

---

## Asset Categories

The Guild’s assets are grouped into seven categories:

1. People
2. Devices
3. Accounts and Access
4. Data and Records
5. Network and Infrastructure
6. Physical Spaces
7. Vendors and Outside Services

Each category matters because each one can become a door, a key, a target, or a weak place in the wall.

---

# 1. People

People are the heart of the Guild.

They are also part of the security picture.

## Students

Students use Guild systems to learn, practice, submit work, access training materials, and build new skills.

**Why they matter:**
Students may have personal information, learning records, login accounts, and assigned devices tied to them.

**Possible risks:**

* Phishing attempts
* Weak passwords
* Lost devices
* Accidental data exposure
* Unsafe public Wi-Fi use
* Clicking malicious links

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Students are users who need security awareness, safe access, and protection of personal data.

---

## Staff

Staff members manage training, student records, scheduling, communications, devices, and internal processes.

**Why they matter:**
Staff accounts may have access to sensitive records, shared drives, student information, and administrative tools.

**Possible risks:**

* Over-permissioned accounts
* Compromised email
* Shared passwords
* Poor offboarding
* Social engineering
* Accidental misconfiguration

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Staff access must follow least privilege, strong authentication, and clear account management.

---

## Volunteers

Volunteers may help teach, repair devices, mentor students, manage events, or assist with operations.

**Why they matter:**
Volunteers may need limited access to Guild systems, but they should not automatically receive full access to sensitive records.

**Possible risks:**

* Too much access
* Unclear role boundaries
* Forgotten accounts after volunteering ends
* Personal devices connecting to Guild systems

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Volunteer access should be temporary, role-based, and reviewed regularly.

---

# 2. Devices

Devices are some of the Guild’s most important anchors.

They connect people to the systems they need.

They also create risk when they are lost, stolen, outdated, or misconfigured.

## Donated Laptops

Donated laptops are used by students for training, practice, labs, and coursework.

**Why they matter:**
A donated laptop may be a student’s only doorway into the digital side of the Wuerld.

**Possible risks:**

* Old operating systems
* Missing patches
* Unknown previous data
* Malware
* Weak local accounts
* No encryption
* Lost or stolen equipment

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Donated laptops need secure wiping, patching, inventory tracking, endpoint protection, and user assignment records.

---

## Staff Laptops

Staff laptops are used for administrative work, communication, student support, and internal documents.

**Why they matter:**
Staff laptops may touch sensitive information and internal systems.

**Possible risks:**

* Phishing
* Stolen device
* Unencrypted storage
* Weak login controls
* Unauthorized software
* Use on unsafe networks

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Staff devices should use encryption, MFA, patching, endpoint protection, and secure configuration.

---

## Shared Lab Computers

Shared lab computers are used by multiple students in training spaces.

**Why they matter:**
Shared machines can collect leftover files, cached logins, browser history, and accidental data from many users.

**Possible risks:**

* Students staying logged in
* Files left behind
* Malware from downloads
* Misconfigured permissions
* Unauthorized software
* Weak local admin controls

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Shared devices need standard images, restricted permissions, regular resets, and clear acceptable use rules.

---

## Network Equipment

Network equipment includes routers, switches, wireless access points, firewalls, and any hardware that keeps the Guild connected.

**Why it matters:**
If the network equipment is weak, everything connected to it becomes easier to attack.

**Possible risks:**

* Default passwords
* Outdated firmware
* Weak Wi-Fi settings
* Poor segmentation
* Misconfigured firewall rules
* Unmonitored traffic

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Network equipment should be securely configured, updated, documented, and reviewed.

---

# 3. Accounts and Access

Accounts are keys.

Some keys open small doors.

Some keys open the whole building.

The Guild must know who has access, what they can reach, and whether they still need it.

## Student Accounts

Student accounts provide access to learning platforms, shared resources, training labs, and communication tools.

**Why they matter:**
Student accounts tie learners to their progress, files, assignments, and identity inside the Guild.

**Possible risks:**

* Weak passwords
* Password reuse
* Forgotten accounts
* Account sharing
* Phishing
* No MFA

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Student accounts need strong password rules, MFA when possible, and proper disabling after program completion.

---

## Staff Accounts

Staff accounts may access internal documents, student information, schedules, shared drives, and administrative systems.

**Why they matter:**
Staff accounts often carry more privilege than student accounts.

**Possible risks:**

* Excessive permissions
* Phished credentials
* Shared admin accounts
* Poor offboarding
* Lack of access reviews

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Staff access should follow least privilege, MFA, role-based access, and regular reviews.

---

## Admin Accounts

Admin accounts control systems, devices, networks, or platforms.

**Why they matter:**
Admin accounts are high-value keys. If stolen, they can become Root Wraiths.

**Possible risks:**

* Shared admin credentials
* No MFA
* Stale admin accounts
* Lack of logging
* Privilege misuse
* Weak password storage

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Admin accounts need strong controls, separate use from daily accounts, MFA, logging, and strict access limits.

---

## Vendor Accounts

Vendor accounts may be used by outside services or support providers.

**Why they matter:**
Vendors can become hidden doors into Guild systems.

**Possible risks:**

* Too much access
* No expiration date
* Poor vendor security
* Lack of monitoring
* Unclear responsibility
* Access that stays active after work ends

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Vendor access should be limited, documented, monitored, and removed when no longer needed.

---

# 4. Data and Records

Data is memory.

Records prove what happened, who belongs, what was learned, and what must be protected.

After the Collision, records are not just paperwork.

They are memory anchors.

## Student Records

Student records may include names, contact information, progress notes, attendance, coursework, and program participation.

**Why they matter:**
These records are sensitive and tied directly to real people.

**Possible risks:**

* Unauthorized access
* Accidental sharing
* Poor storage controls
* Weak permissions
* Unencrypted files
* Retention problems

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Student records require confidentiality, access control, data classification, and retention rules.

---

## Training Materials

Training materials include lessons, slides, labs, worksheets, videos, guides, and cybersecurity exercises.

**Why they matter:**
Training materials support learning and may include internal methods, lab credentials, or simulated attack/defense content.

**Possible risks:**

* Unauthorized editing
* Loss of materials
* Exposed lab credentials
* Confusing outdated versions
* Public sharing of internal content

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Training materials need version control, backup, access management, and review.

---

## Internal Documents

Internal documents include policies, procedures, schedules, planning notes, budgets, meeting notes, and operational records.

**Why they matter:**
Internal documents explain how the Guild works and may reveal sensitive processes.

**Possible risks:**

* Oversharing
* Poor folder permissions
* Lost files
* No backups
* Sensitive information stored casually

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Internal documents need classification, proper storage, least privilege, and backup.

---

## Security Logs

Security logs include login records, alerts, device events, network activity, failed access attempts, and system changes.

**Why they matter:**
Logs are the footprints of the Guild.

They help Logkeepers see what happened before, during, and after an incident.

**Possible risks:**

* Logs not collected
* Logs deleted too soon
* Logs nobody reviews
* Logs stored without protection
* Alerts ignored

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Security logs support detection, investigation, incident response, and accountability.

---

# 5. Network and Infrastructure

The Guild’s network is the road system.

If the roads are unsafe, every traveler is at risk.

## Guild Wi-Fi

Guild Wi-Fi may support students, staff, volunteers, guests, and training devices.

**Why it matters:**
Wi-Fi is often the first door people use to enter the Guild’s systems.

**Possible risks:**

* Weak Wi-Fi password
* Shared password never rotated
* No guest network
* Students and staff on same network
* Rogue access points
* Unclear acceptable use

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Wi-Fi should use strong encryption, segmentation, password rotation, and guest isolation.

---

## Guest Network

The guest network allows visitors or temporary users to connect without touching sensitive internal systems.

**Why it matters:**
Guests need access, but not all access should lead deeper into the Guild.

**Possible risks:**

* Guest users reaching internal systems
* Weak captive portal
* No bandwidth or access controls
* Unknown devices on the network

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Guest networks should be separated from internal resources.

---

## Shared Drives or Cloud Storage

Shared storage may hold student records, training materials, policies, and internal documents.

**Why it matters:**
Shared storage becomes risky when permissions are messy.

**Possible risks:**

* Everyone has access to everything
* Sensitive files in public folders
* No version history
* No backup
* Files shared with personal accounts

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Shared storage needs access control, file classification, backup, and periodic permission reviews.

---

## Email System

Email is used for staff communication, student updates, vendor contact, scheduling, and documents.

**Why it matters:**
Email is where many Phishing Sirens sing first.

**Possible risks:**

* Phishing
* Malware attachments
* Credential theft
* Fake invoices
* Spoofed messages
* Sensitive information sent to the wrong person

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Email needs phishing awareness, MFA, filtering, reporting procedures, and safe handling rules.

---

# 6. Physical Spaces

The Guild is not only digital.

The physical spaces matter too.

A person standing in the wrong room can become a cybersecurity problem.

## Training Lab

The training lab holds shared computers, student workstations, networking equipment, whiteboards, notes, and learning materials.

**Why it matters:**
Labs are active learning spaces with many users and many chances for accidental exposure.

**Possible risks:**

* Devices left unlocked
* Passwords written down
* Students accessing each other’s files
* Equipment moved without tracking
* Unauthorized USB devices
* Physical theft

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Physical security supports device security, account security, and data protection.

---

## Staff Office

The staff office may contain staff laptops, printed records, schedules, files, and administrative systems.

**Why it matters:**
Staff areas may hold sensitive information.

**Possible risks:**

* Unlocked screens
* Exposed paperwork
* Visitors entering restricted areas
* Devices left unattended
* Poor clean desk habits

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Staff spaces need access limits, screen locking, document handling rules, and physical awareness.

---

## Equipment Storage

Equipment storage holds donated laptops, spare devices, cables, networking gear, and repair tools.

**Why it matters:**
Untracked equipment can disappear, be misused, or re-enter the Guild without being secured.

**Possible risks:**

* Missing laptops
* Unknown device condition
* Old drives with data
* No checkout process
* Unauthorized access

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Equipment storage needs inventory tracking, secure wiping, checkout records, and physical controls.

---

# 7. Vendors and Outside Services

The Guild cannot do everything alone.

Outside services may help with software, cloud platforms, internet service, repairs, donations, training tools, or payment processing.

But every outside connection becomes part of the Guild’s risk.

## Internet Service Provider

The ISP provides connectivity to the Guild.

**Why it matters:**
Without internet access, the Guild loses access to cloud tools, training platforms, email, and communication.

**Possible risks:**

* Outages
* Weak router configuration
* Poor support visibility
* Unclear responsibility for equipment
* No backup connectivity

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Internet service is part of business continuity, network security, and vendor risk.

---

## Cloud Learning Platforms

Cloud learning platforms may host courses, assignments, labs, or student progress.

**Why they matter:**
These platforms may contain student data and learning records.

**Possible risks:**

* Weak access controls
* Poor privacy settings
* Vendor breach
* Account compromise
* Data export problems

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Cloud platforms require vendor review, access management, MFA, and data protection.

---

## Device Repair or Donation Partners

Repair partners or donors may provide laptops, parts, or technical support.

**Why they matter:**
Donated or repaired devices must be trusted before entering the Guild environment.

**Possible risks:**

* Devices with old data
* Malware on donated equipment
* Unknown hardware condition
* No chain of custody
* Missing documentation

**Cybersecurity meaning:**
Device intake needs wiping, inspection, inventory, and secure setup.

---

# First Protection Priorities

The Guild should protect the highest-risk anchors first.

## Priority 1: Student Records

Student records are sensitive and tied to real people.

**Needed controls:**

* Limit access to staff who need it
* Store records in approved locations
* Use MFA where possible
* Avoid storing sensitive records on shared lab machines
* Back up important records
* Review permissions regularly

---

## Priority 2: Staff and Admin Accounts

Staff and admin accounts can open important doors.

**Needed controls:**

* Require strong passwords
* Use MFA
* Avoid shared admin accounts
* Remove access when people leave
* Review permissions
* Log administrative activity

---

## Priority 3: Donated and Shared Devices

Devices can carry old data, malware, or weak settings.

**Needed controls:**

* Wipe donated devices before use
* Patch operating systems
* Track device assignments
* Use endpoint protection where possible
* Disable unnecessary local admin access
* Encrypt staff devices when possible

---

## Priority 4: Wi-Fi and Network Equipment

The network is the Guild’s signal road.

**Needed controls:**

* Change default router passwords
* Use strong Wi-Fi encryption
* Separate guest and internal networks
* Update firmware
* Document network equipment
* Review firewall rules

---

## Priority 5: Security Awareness

People are part of the defense.

**Needed controls:**

* Teach phishing awareness
* Explain password safety
* Teach safe public Wi-Fi habits
* Encourage reporting suspicious messages
* Make security plain-language and non-shaming
